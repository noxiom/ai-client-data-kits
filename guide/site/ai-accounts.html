Personal or work account? What AI tools do with your business data

What happens to client data in ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, Claude or DeepSeek depends less on the tool than on the account someone is signed in to.

Personal account

Free or paid, signed up by the employee

  • ✕No processor agreement with your firm
  • ✕Most train on what people type, unless it is switched off
  • ✕Your firm can't see, manage or delete it

Keep client data out

Work account

Bought and managed by the firm

  • ✓A processor agreement (DPA) with your firm
  • ✓No training on your data by default
  • ✓Your admins control access, retention and settings

Can carry client data, within your own rules

! A work plan only covers the people who actually use it. In the Netherlands, personal data put into a chatbot against your firm's agreements counts as a data breach.1

Check your own team in 20 minutes

The step most firms skip: finding out which accounts people really use.

1

Billing

Look for AI subscriptions on card statements and expense claims.

What to look for

OpenAI, Anthropic, Google One or Google AI, Microsoft 365 Personal or Family. Each one is a personal account being used for work.

2

Admin screens

See what your own admin consoles already show.

Where to look
  • Microsoft 365: the Copilot Chat usage report, and the web search setting.
  • Google Workspace: whether the Gemini app is on, and how long chats are kept.
  • ChatGPT Business or Claude Team: the member list next to your staff list.
3

Ask

Six anonymous questions, in your own form tool.

The six questions
  1. In the last month, which AI tools have you used for work?
  2. For each: work account, personal account, or not signed in?
  3. Do you pay for any yourself, or claim them as expenses?
  4. What have you put in? Nothing about clients / client names / client documents or emails / financial or HR information / health or other sensitive information / not sure.
  5. Which AI tool would you use more if the firm provided it?
  6. Is there anything you're unsure is allowed?

Say plainly that honest answers have no consequences: you want the real picture.

Client data already went into a personal account? 6 steps
  1. Write it down now: which tool and account, when, what data and whose, who entered it, what has been done since. The GDPR requires you to document every personal data breach, including ones you don't report.2
  2. Remove what you can: delete the conversation and, if training was on, switch it off. Each tool page says what that vendor does after deletion.
  3. Assess the risk to the people whose data it was. Health information, identity numbers or financial details weigh heavier than a name and an email address.
  4. Decide on notification within 72 hours. Unless the breach is unlikely to put those people at any risk, it must be reported to the data protection authority without undue delay and within 72 hours of becoming aware of it; if the risk is high, the people affected must be told too.2 The Dutch authority says reporting is mandatory in many chatbot cases.1
  5. Check your client contracts for clauses on reporting incidents to the client.
  6. Fix the cause: a work account and clear rules about what may go in.

Step by step, with a record you can fill in and save: the first 72 hours. For staff: what to do if you pasted something.

For the notification decision itself, involve your privacy officer or a privacy lawyer. This guide is not legal advice.

Questions

Is ChatGPT (or Copilot, Gemini, Claude) GDPR compliant?

No tool is GDPR compliant or non-compliant by itself. The GDPR applies to how your firm uses it. What a vendor can give you is a processor agreement (GDPR Article 28), no training on your data, admin control and clear storage terms. Work plans offer those; personal accounts don't, because the contract is between the vendor and the individual.2

Is it safe to put company data into ChatGPT, Copilot or Gemini?

It depends on the account someone is signed in to, not on the brand. A personal account has no contract with your firm, and most personal accounts may use what is typed for training unless it is switched off. Work accounts (ChatGPT Business, Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat with a work login, Gemini in Google Workspace) don't train on your data by default and come with a processing agreement. Which data may go in is then your firm's decision. The comparison above shows each account, with sources.3, 4, 5

Is it a data breach if an employee puts client data into ChatGPT?

In the Netherlands, the Dutch data protection authority (Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens) says that if employees enter personal data into a chatbot contrary to the agreements made with their employer, that is a data breach. If chatbot use is the organisation's own policy, it is not a breach, but it is often not permitted by law. Notifying the authority and the people affected is mandatory in many cases.1

Does buying a business plan solve it?

Only for the people who use it. A business plan covers work accounts. It does nothing about a colleague who keeps using a personal account on the same laptop, and from the outside you often can't tell which one was used. That's why the check below starts with asking.

Can we just block personal AI accounts?

Partly. Microsoft lets admins stop personal-account sign-in to Copilot on company devices with tenant restrictions, and network filters can block whole sites. None of that reaches personal phones or home computers, so clear rules and a work account people actually like using matter more than blocking.4

About this guide

What it does and doesn't tell you

It tells you what each vendor publicly states about each account type on the date shown, where vendors are silent, and where their own pages contradict each other.

It doesn't verify that vendors keep their promises (we read their terms; we don't audit them), cover negotiated enterprise contracts or every setting, tell you whether your firm's use is lawful, or show which tools and accounts your team actually uses. It is not legal advice.

What we couldn't confirm
  • Consumer Copilot (the app updated on 18 August 2026): Microsoft's pages don't state the retention period, human review or data location.
  • ChatGPT Business: OpenAI doesn't list which storage regions are offered, and its pages disagree on whether admins can read members' chats.
  • Claude Team and Enterprise: we found no EU data residency option.
  • DeepSeek: an in-app training opt-out is described by third parties only; the policy describes opting out by email.
  • Microsoft 365 Copilot: only the Copilot Business price was checked, from a March 2026 Microsoft post; Microsoft's pricing page did not load.
