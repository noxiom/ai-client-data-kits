The step most firms skip: finding out which accounts people really use.

Billing

Look for AI subscriptions on card statements and expense claims.

What to look for

OpenAI, Anthropic, Google One or Google AI, Microsoft 365 Personal or Family. Each one is a personal account being used for work.

2

Admin screens

See what your own admin consoles already show.

Where to look
  • Microsoft 365: the Copilot Chat usage report, and the web search setting.
  • Google Workspace: whether the Gemini app is on, and how long chats are kept.
  • ChatGPT Business or Claude Team: the member list next to your staff list.
3

Ask

Six anonymous questions, in your own form tool.

The six questions
  1. In the last month, which AI tools have you used for work?
  2. For each: work account, personal account, or not signed in?
  3. Do you pay for any yourself, or claim them as expenses?
  4. What have you put in? Nothing about clients / client names / client documents or emails / financial or HR information / health or other sensitive information / not sure.
  5. Which AI tool would you use more if the firm provided it?
  6. Is there anything you're unsure is allowed?

Say plainly that honest answers have no consequences: you want the real picture.

Client data already went into a personal account? 6 steps
  1. Write it down now: which tool and account, when, what data and whose, who entered it, what has been done since. The GDPR requires you to document every personal data breach, including ones you don't report.[[cite:gdpr]]
  2. Remove what you can: delete the conversation and, if training was on, switch it off. Each tool page says what that vendor does after deletion.
  3. Assess the risk to the people whose data it was. Health information, identity numbers or financial details weigh heavier than a name and an email address.
  4. Decide on notification within 72 hours. Unless the breach is unlikely to put those people at any risk, it must be reported to the data protection authority without undue delay and within 72 hours of becoming aware of it; if the risk is high, the people affected must be told too.[[cite:gdpr]] The Dutch authority says reporting is mandatory in many chatbot cases.[[cite:ap-chatbots]]
  5. Check your client contracts for clauses on reporting incidents to the client.
  6. Fix the cause: a work account and clear rules about what may go in.

Step by step, with a record you can fill in and save: the first 72 hours. For staff: what to do if you pasted something.

For the notification decision itself, involve your privacy officer or a privacy lawyer. This guide is not legal advice.

About this guide

What it does and doesn't tell you

It tells you what each vendor publicly states about each account type on the date shown, where vendors are silent, and where their own pages contradict each other.

It doesn't verify that vendors keep their promises (we read their terms; we don't audit them), cover negotiated enterprise contracts or every setting, tell you whether your firm's use is lawful, or show which tools and accounts your team actually uses. It is not legal advice.

Why the account matters more than the brand

Five different things get called "using ChatGPT". Vendors' promises depend on the plan, and only apply to the account someone actually signs in to.

LayerExampleWho decides
The toolChatGPT, Copilot, GeminiThe vendor
The accountA colleague's personal login, or the firm's workspaceWhoever signs in
The planFree, Plus, Business, EnterpriseWhoever pays
The settingsTraining on or off, how long chats are kept, web searchThe user, or your admin on work plans
