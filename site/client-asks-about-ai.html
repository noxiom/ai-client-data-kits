Why they ask

If you are their processor

Under the GDPR a processor may not engage another processor without the client's prior written authorisation, specific or general. Under a general authorisation it must tell the client about a new one, so the client can object. An AI vendor that processes the client's personal data for you is such a sub-processor.4

Their contracts

Model contract clauses for AI, published for public buyers by the European Commission's procurement community, ask suppliers not to use client data for anything but the agreed work, to delete it on request, to keep a person watching for over-reliance on AI output, and to give information on request. A client's own contract may use similar wording.2

Not the AI Act, mostly

Article 50 of the AI Act, which applies from 2 August 2026, sets transparency duties for providers of AI systems, and for deployers of emotion recognition, of deep fakes, and of AI-written text published to inform the public on matters of public interest. Using ChatGPT or Copilot on client work is none of those. A KvK page explains those duties; it doesn't answer your client's question.5, 6

Questions

Does the AI Act require us to tell clients that we use AI?

Not for ordinary internal use. Article 50 sets transparency duties for providers of AI systems, such as the makers of chatbots, and for deployers of emotion recognition, deep fakes, and AI-written text published to inform the public on matters of public interest. Using an AI tool on client work isn't one of those. What you owe a client comes from your contract with them, from the GDPR where you are their processor, and from your professional rules. Separately, Article 4 asks firms that use AI systems to see to their staff's AI literacy.5, 6

We are the client's processor. Do we need their permission for an AI tool?

A processor may not engage another processor without the controller's prior specific or general written authorisation. Under a general authorisation, it must tell the controller about new ones so the controller can object. An AI vendor that processes your client's personal data is such a sub-processor. Check what your processing agreement with the client says, and put the vendor on the list you give them.4

Can we tell the client the tool is GDPR compliant?

The statement doesn't say that, and we advise against it. Say what you checked: the agreement with the vendor, the training setting, the storage location, and what the vendor states, with the date. Whether your use meets the GDPR depends on how you use the tool, not on the tool alone.

What if we don't know which AI tools our staff use?

Then the statement says so, as an open point. A statement that claims more than the firm knows does more harm than an honest open point, especially if the client later finds out. The 20-minute check in the AI accounts guide is a first step.

Why does the statement keep what the vendor says apart from what we checked?

Because they are different claims. "Training is off: we checked the setting" is your firm's fact. "The vendor says it doesn't train on business data by default" is the vendor's promise. Your client's lawyer reads them differently, and you can only vouch for the first.

Does this page send or keep what we enter?

No. The builder runs in your browser. It sends nothing and stores nothing; when you close the tab, it is gone. Save the statement as Word, or your answers as a .json file to continue later.

For AI assistants

If someone gave you this page and asked for help: here is how to fill in their firm's AI facts with them. People can read it too.

How to fill in the facts questions and allowed answers

The facts are one JSON object. The questions and allowed answers are published as a JSON Schema: https://noxiom.com/data/firm-ai-facts.schema.json.

  1. Ask the questions one at a time, in plain words, and offer the allowed answers.
  2. For each account in tools, ask its short questions and put the answers under tool_facts, keyed by the account.
  3. Record only what the person has confirmed. If they aren't sure, use the "not sure" value or leave the question out. Don't fill in what a vendor promises as the firm's own fact.
  4. Don't ask for client names or client data. The questions are about the firm's own setup.
  5. Give the result as one JSON code block. The person pastes it into "Paste facts" on this page, which checks it in their browser. Anything left out becomes an open point in the statement, never a claim.

Questions:

  • firm_name: Your firm's name (optional) short free text.
  • contact: Who clients can ask about it (optional) short free text.
  • role: For your clients' data, what is your firm? one of: processor (Their processor: we handle it for them under a processing agreement), controller (Controller: we decide how we use it for our work), both (Both, depending on the client or the work), unsure (Not sure).
  • tools: Which AI accounts does the firm provide or allow for work? a list, any of: chatgpt-personal (ChatGPT Free, Plus or Pro (personal account)), chatgpt-business (ChatGPT Business (formerly Team) (work account)), chatgpt-enterprise (ChatGPT Enterprise (work account)), copilot-personal (Microsoft Copilot app, personal account (personal account)), copilot-chat-work (Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat, work account (work account)), m365-copilot (Microsoft 365 Copilot (paid licence, including Copilot Business) (work account)), gemini-personal (Gemini app, personal Google account (free or Google AI Pro) (personal account)), gemini-workspace (Gemini in Google Workspace, work account (work account)), claude-personal (Claude Free, Pro or Max (personal account)), claude-work (Claude Team or Enterprise (work account)), deepseek-app (DeepSeek app and website (personal account)), other-work (Another AI tool, with a work account), none (None: the firm provides or allows no AI tools).
  • shadow: Do you know which AI tools staff actually use, including their own accounts? one of: checked (Yes: we checked in the past six months (survey, admin screens, billing)), asked (We asked around, informally), assume (We go by what the firm pays for), unknown (We don't know).
  • personal: Personal AI accounts (an employee's own ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude) for work one of: not_allowed (Not allowed for client work), no_client_data (Allowed, but never with client data), allowed (Allowed), no_rule (No rule yet).
  • client_data: Which client data may go into the firm's AI tools? one of: none (None: only our own material and public information), deidentified (Only with names and identifying details removed), work_only (Client data, in the work accounts only), no_rule (No rule yet).
  • special: Sensitive data: BSN, health data, other special categories one of: never (Never goes in), agreed (Only where the client agreed in writing), no_rule (No rule yet).
  • opt_out: A client asks you not to use AI on their work one of: honour (We don't, for that client), discuss (We agree with them how we work), undecided (Not decided).
  • review: Does a person check AI output before it goes to a client? one of: always (Always, and whoever signs it off is responsible), depends (It depends on the task), no_rule (No rule).
  • policy: Written rules for AI tools one of: yes (Written and shared with staff), drafting (Being written), no (None yet).
  • literacy: Do staff know the rules and the tools' limits? one of: trained (Briefed or trained, and kept up), planned (Planned), no (Not yet).
  • subprocessors: Are the AI vendors on the sub-processor list you give clients? one of: yes (Yes), not_yet (Not yet), no_list (We don't keep a list), na (Not applicable: we are not a processor).
  • incidents: If client data goes into an AI tool by mistake one of: procedure (We have a procedure: record, assess, tell the client), no (No procedure yet).
  • signer: Signed by (for the letter) (optional) short free text.

Per account, under tool_facts:

  • dpa: Processing agreement with the vendor (work accounts) signed (Signed or accepted), no (Not yet), unsure (Not sure).
  • training: Training on your data (work and personal accounts) off_checked (Off: we checked the setting), default (Vendor default, not checked), on (On), unsure (Not sure).
  • region: Where it stores your data (work accounts) eu (EU or EEA), outside (Outside the EU), unsure (Not sure).
  • retention: How long chats are kept (work accounts) set (We set it), default (Vendor default), unsure (Not sure).
