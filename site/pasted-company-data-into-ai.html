I put company information into an AI tool. What now?

It happens, and it can be handled. What matters most is that the right person at your firm hears about it today, from you.

Checked 23 November 2026 A two-minute read For the person you tell

In short

  1. Write down what went in and where, without copying the information itself.
  2. Tell the person your firm has named for this today: your manager, office manager or privacy contact.
  3. Then delete the conversation, unless your firm asks you to wait.

! Why today? If personal data was involved, your firm may have to decide within 72 hours of finding out whether to report it to the Dutch data protection authority (the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens). It has to keep a record of every incident like this, including the ones it doesn't report. It can only do either if it knows.1, 2

Three steps, in this order

1 Now, 5 minutes

Note it

Describe what happened. Don't paste the information again anywhere, not even into an email to your manager.

  • Which tool, and which account: your personal account, or a work account your firm gave you.
  • When, roughly.
  • What kind of information, and whose: for example "a client's payroll file with about 40 names and salaries".
  • Pasted text, an uploaded file, or a connected mailbox or drive.
  • Whether you shared the chat with a link, and whether the tool's training setting was on.
2 Today

Tell your firm

Tell your manager, office manager or the privacy contact. A short message is enough; you can copy the one below.

Hi [name], I need to report something. On [date] at about [time] I put [describe the kind of information, not the information itself] from [a client / our own files] into [tool], using my [personal / work] account. I have [not yet] deleted the conversation. Can we talk today?

3 After you've noted it

Delete it

Delete the conversation and remove any share link. If the training setting was on, switch it off. Wait first if your firm asks you to: it may want to see the chat for its record.

What deleting does, per tool
  • ChatGPT: In a personal account, delete the conversation: OpenAI schedules deleted chats for permanent deletion within 30 days. Check whether "Improve the model for everyone" was on at the time, and note it in your record.3, 4 More on ChatGPT
  • Microsoft Copilot: In the consumer app, delete the conversation from the chat list. Microsoft's pages for the updated app don't state how long deleted or kept chats are retained, so record that you could not confirm it.5 More on Microsoft Copilot
  • Gemini: In a personal account, delete the chat from Gemini's history. Note that chats already selected for human review are kept for up to three years and aren't deleted along with the history.6 More on Gemini
  • Claude: In a personal account, delete the chat: Anthropic deletes it from back-end storage within 30 days. If the Model Improvement setting was on, de-identified data may already be in training pipelines, where Anthropic says it can be kept for up to five years. Record which it was.7, 8 More on Claude
  • DeepSeek: Delete the chat history in DeepSeek's settings. DeepSeek's policy says users exercise their privacy rights, including the training opt-out, by emailing privacy@deepseek.com. Record what you asked and when.9 More on DeepSeek
Work account

If your firm gave you a work account (for example ChatGPT Business, or Copilot with the green shield) and its rules allow this kind of information in it, this may not be an incident at all. The Dutch data protection authority says that when chatbot use is part of an organisation's policy, it is not a data breach. If you're not sure what the rules allow, tell your manager anyway.10

What happens next

  1. Your firm writes the incident down.
  2. It assesses the risk to the people whose information it was.
  3. It decides whether to report it to the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, and whether to tell the client and the people concerned.
  4. You may be asked for details. The decisions are the firm's, not yours.

Questions

Is it a data breach if I put client data into ChatGPT?

If it included personal data and you used the tool against your employer's agreements, the Dutch data protection authority says it is a data breach. Not every breach has to be reported to the authority: only when it is likely to put the people concerned at some risk. Your firm makes that call.10, 1

Should I delete the conversation straight away?

Note what went in first, and tell your firm. Then delete it, unless your firm asks you to wait because it wants to see the chat for its record. Deleting doesn't always remove everything: some tools keep deleted chats for a while, and anything already used for training stays there. The per-tool notes on this page say what each vendor does.

I pasted a password or an access key into an AI tool.

Change the password or replace the key now, everywhere it is used, and tell whoever manages your IT. Then delete the conversation. A changed password is worthless to anyone who sees the chat later.

It was company information, but nothing about people. Does it still matter?

It isn't a personal data breach under the GDPR, because no personal data was involved. It can still matter: client contracts often require confidentiality, and the tool may keep or train on what you typed. Tell your manager so the firm can check.1

Can my employer see what I typed into ChatGPT?

Not in your personal account: your firm has no access to it. In a work account it depends on the plan and the settings. The AI accounts guide shows what admins can see per tool.

Will I get into trouble?

This page can't speak for your employer. What it can say: your firm needs to know to meet its own obligations, and the sooner it knows, the more options it has.

About this page

What it does and doesn't tell you

It tells you what to do as an employee, and what each AI vendor says happens to a deleted conversation, as checked in the AI accounts guide on 22 November 2026.

It doesn't replace your firm's own rules, decide whether anything must be reported, or give legal advice. If your firm has its own procedure, follow that.
