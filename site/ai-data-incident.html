Client data went into an AI tool: the first 72 hours

Someone at your firm put client or personal data into ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, Claude or another AI tool. Here is what to do, in order, with a record you can fill in and save.

Checked 23 November 2026 Record builder: runs in your browser Templates For staff

In short

Write it down, contain it, check whether you are the controller or the client's processor, weigh the risk to the people concerned, and decide within 72 hours of becoming aware whether to report it to the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Record the decision either way.

The first 72 hours, step by step

1 First hour

Write it down

Which tool and account, when, what kind of data and whose, how it went in, who found it. Describe the data; don't copy it.

Why and how

The GDPR requires a record of every personal data breach, including the ones you don't report: the facts, the effects and what you did about it. The record builder below produces one.1, 2

2 First hour

Contain it

Delete the conversation, remove share links, switch off training, change any password or key that went in.

Why and how

What deletion does differs per vendor and per account. Choose the tool and account in the record builder to see it, with sources.

3 Day 1

Check your role

Is this your firm's own data, or do you handle it for a client under a processing agreement?

Why and how

If you are the client's processor, tell the client without undue delay. The client assesses the risk and decides about the authority; your processing agreement may set its own deadline. If you are the controller, the next steps are yours.2

4 Day 1 to 2

Weigh the risk

To the people whose data it was: how sensitive, how many, can it be undone, can the recipient be relied on.

Why and how

These are the criteria the European data protection authorities use. The record builder shows which of your answers point towards reporting and which may lower the risk. It never decides for you.2

5 Within 72 hours

Decide and record why

Report to the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens unless the breach is unlikely to put anyone at risk. Tell the people concerned too if the risk is high.

Why and how

The 72 hours run from when your firm became reasonably certain that personal data was compromised. If you don't have every detail yet, you can report in phases. Record the decision and the reasons, whichever way it goes.1, 2, 3

6 After

Fix the cause

A work account for the people who need one, clear rules about which data may go in, and a short word with the team.

Why and how

One incident is usually the first one you hear about, not the only one. The AI accounts guide has a 20-minute check of which accounts your team really uses.

What points towards reporting, and what doesn't

The record builder uses these factors. They follow the criteria the European data protection authorities use to weigh the risk to the people concerned.2

All factors with sources

✕A problem for client data: Point towards reporting

  • Who received it. A personal account has no processor agreement with your firm, so the vendor isn't bound by your instructions about this data.2
  • Can it be undone. Training was on, or may have been. The vendor may use the data to train its models, and deleting the chat may not undo that.2
  • Is it a breach. The Dutch authority says personal data entered into a chatbot against the employer's agreements is a data breach.4
  • Sensitivity of the data. Health, other special categories or criminal data: the harm to the people concerned can be especially severe.2
  • Sensitivity of the data. Identity numbers or financial details can cause harm on their own, and together they can be used for identity theft.2
  • Who the people are. Data about children: the people concerned may be at greater risk.2
  • Sensitivity of the data. CVs and assessments often reveal more about a person than they seem to.2
  • Severity of the consequences. Passwords or keys open other systems. Until they are changed, the risk reaches everything they give access to.
  • Number of people. More people affected generally means more impact.2
  • How far it spread. A share link may let anyone who has it read the chat. Remove it and record when.
  • If in doubt. You can't rule out sensitive data yet. The European guidance is to report when in doubt.2

✓In your favour: May lower the risk

  • Who received it. A work account under a processor agreement binds the vendor to your firm's instructions, if your firm accepted or signed that agreement. A recipient you can rely on can lower the risk; it doesn't mean nothing happened.2
  • Is it a breach. Within your own rules, the Dutch authority doesn't treat chatbot use as a data breach, though it says such use is often not permitted by law. Record it and check the basis for the use.4
  • Sensitivity of the data. Only names and contact details of a few people. In ordinary circumstances that is unlikely to cause substantial damage, though context can change that.2
  • Is it a breach. No personal data: this is not a personal data breach under the GDPR. Check your client contract's confidentiality clause all the same.1
  • Can it be undone. Deleted, with training off: this lowers the chance of further use. It doesn't undo what the vendor has already received.2

?The vendor doesn't say: Still to find out

  • Who received it. Find out which account it was. It decides whether a contract covers the data.
  • Number of people. Estimate how many people's data it was. The number matters, though even one person can be seriously affected.2
  • Is it a breach. The Dutch authority's statement is about use against the employer's agreements. Without clear rules, your firm has to judge whether this use was authorised.4
  • Who decides. Check your processing agreement with the client. If you are their processor, your duty is to tell them without undue delay, and they decide about reporting.2

For AI assistants

If someone gave you this page and asked for help: here is how to fill in the record with them. People can read it too.

How to fill in the record questions and allowed answers

The record is one JSON object. The full list of questions and allowed answers is published as a JSON Schema: https://noxiom.com/data/ai-incident-record.schema.json.

  1. Ask the questions one at a time, in plain words, and offer the allowed answers.
  2. Describe the data by type only. Never ask for the data itself, or for the names of the people or the client.
  3. Use only the listed values. If the person doesn't know, use the "not sure" value or leave the question out. Don't guess.
  4. Don't decide whether the breach must be reported. The page shows the factors; the firm decides.
  5. Give the result as one JSON code block. The person pastes it into "Paste a record" on this page, which checks it and fills the form in their browser.

Fields:

  • aware_at: When did the firm become aware? local time, YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM.
  • reported_by: How was it found? one of: self (They reported it themselves), colleague (A colleague noticed), outside (A client or outsider told us), check (We found it in a check).
  • happened_at: When did it happen (roughly)? (optional) local time, YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM.
  • plan: Which tool and account? one of: chatgpt-personal (ChatGPT Free, Plus or Pro (personal account)), chatgpt-business (ChatGPT Business (formerly Team) (work account)), chatgpt-enterprise (ChatGPT Enterprise (work account)), copilot-personal (Microsoft Copilot app, personal account (personal account)), copilot-chat-work (Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat, work account (work account)), m365-copilot (Microsoft 365 Copilot (paid licence, including Copilot Business) (work account)), gemini-personal (Gemini app, personal Google account (free or Google AI Pro) (personal account)), gemini-workspace (Gemini in Google Workspace, work account (work account)), claude-personal (Claude Free, Pro or Max (personal account)), claude-work (Claude Team or Enterprise (work account)), deepseek-app (DeepSeek app and website (personal account)), other-personal (Another AI tool: a personal or free account), other-work (Another AI tool: a work account with a contract), unknown (Not sure which tool or account).
  • how: How did it go in? one of: pasted (Pasted or typed text), upload (Uploaded a file), connected (Connected mailbox or drive), meeting (Meeting recording or transcript), other (Something else).
  • rules: Was this within your rules for AI tools? one of: allowed (Allowed under our rules), against (Against our rules), none (We have no rules on this), unsure (Not sure).
  • training: Was the tool's training setting on at the time? one of: on (On), off (Off, or a temporary chat), unsure (Not sure), na (Not applicable: work account).
  • shared: Was the chat shared with a link? one of: yes (Yes), no (No), unsure (Not sure).
  • categories: What kind of data went in? a list, any of: contact (Names and contact details), financial (Financial details: bank account, salary, invoices, debts), id_numbers (Identity numbers or documents: BSN, passport, driving licence), health (Health information, including sick leave), special (Other special categories: ethnic origin, religion, political views, trade union membership, sexual orientation, biometric or genetic data), criminal (Criminal records or offences), children (Data about children), hr (Employee or job applicant information: CVs, assessments), credentials (Passwords, access keys or tokens), business (Confidential business information, no personal data), unsure (Not sure what was in it).
  • people: About how many people's data? one of: 1 (1), 2-10 (2 to 10), 11-100 (11 to 100), 101-1000 (101 to 1,000), 1000+ (More than 1,000), unknown (Not known yet).
  • whose: Whose data? a list, any of: client (A client's customers or staff), staff (Our own staff), applicants (Job applicants), other (Someone else).
  • description: Short description (optional) free text, by type only.
  • role: For this data, is your firm the controller or a processor? one of: controller (Controller: our own data, or we decide what happens with it), processor (Processor: we handle it for a client under a processing agreement), unsure (Not sure).
  • actions: What has been done? Tick each step as it happens; the time is added to the record. an object of step: local time, steps: deleted (Conversation deleted), link (Share link removed), training_off (Training setting switched off), secrets (Passwords or keys that went in changed), vendor (Vendor asked to delete the data, where it offers that), client (Client informed), contact (Privacy officer or adviser involved).
  • decision: Report to the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens? one of: report (Yes, reported), no (No: unlikely to result in a risk to the people concerned), client (Not ours to decide: we are the processor and told the client), open (Not decided yet).
  • individuals: Tell the people concerned? one of: yes (Yes), no (No: the risk is not high), open (Not decided yet).
  • reasons: Reasons for the decision free text, by type only.
  • decided_by: Decided by (role) free text, by type only.
  • fix: What changes so it doesn't happen again (optional) free text, by type only.

Questions

Do we have to report it to the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens?

Only you can decide that. The rule: report within 72 hours of becoming aware, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the people concerned. The Dutch authority says reporting is mandatory in many chatbot cases. The European guidance says that if in doubt, you should report.1, 4, 2

When do the 72 hours start?

When your firm has a reasonable degree of certainty that personal data was compromised. A short first investigation to establish that doesn't count, but it should start straight away.2

We handle this data for a client. Who reports?

If you are the client's processor, your duty is to tell the client without undue delay. You don't have to assess the risk first; the client does that and decides about reporting. Your processing agreement may set a shorter deadline.2

We decided not to report. Do we still need a record?

Yes. The GDPR requires a record of every personal data breach, including the facts, the effects and what you did. The authority can ask to see it.1, 2

Was it a breach if the employee used a work account we pay for?

If using it with this data was within your rules, the Dutch authority doesn't treat it as a data breach, though it says such use is often not permitted by law. Record what happened and check that the use had a proper basis.4

Does this page send or keep what I type?

No. The record builder runs in your browser. It sends nothing and stores nothing; when you close the tab, it is gone. Save the record as a file or print it before you close.

About this kit

What it does and doesn't do

It gives you the order of steps, the rules they come from, the criteria for weighing the risk, what each AI vendor says about the account involved (from the AI accounts guide, checked 22 November 2026), and a record to keep.

It doesn't decide whether you must report, assess your specific case, or replace a privacy officer or lawyer. For the decision itself, get advice. It is written for firms in the Netherlands; the GDPR rules are the same across the EU, but the authority you report to differs. It is not legal advice.
