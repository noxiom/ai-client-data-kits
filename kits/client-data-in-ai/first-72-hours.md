# Client data went into an AI tool: the first 72 hours

Write it down, contain it, check whether you are the controller or the client's processor, weigh the risk to the people concerned, and decide within 72 hours of becoming aware whether to report it to the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Record the decision either way.

## 1. Write it down (first hour)

Which tool and account, when, what kind of data and whose, how it went in, who found it. Describe the data; don't copy it.

The GDPR requires a record of every personal data breach, including the ones you don't report: the facts, the effects and what you did about it. The record builder below produces one.

## 2. Contain it (first hour)

Delete the conversation, remove share links, switch off training, change any password or key that went in.

What deletion does differs per vendor and per account. Choose the tool and account in the record builder to see it, with sources.

## 3. Check your role (day 1)

Is this your firm's own data, or do you handle it for a client under a processing agreement?

If you are the client's processor, tell the client without undue delay. The client assesses the risk and decides about the authority; your processing agreement may set its own deadline. If you are the controller, the next steps are yours.

## 4. Weigh the risk (day 1 to 2)

To the people whose data it was: how sensitive, how many, can it be undone, can the recipient be relied on.

These are the criteria the European data protection authorities use. The record builder shows which of your answers point towards reporting and which may lower the risk. It never decides for you.

## 5. Decide and record why (within 72 hours)

Report to the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens unless the breach is unlikely to put anyone at risk. Tell the people concerned too if the risk is high.

The 72 hours run from when your firm became reasonably certain that personal data was compromised. If you don't have every detail yet, you can report in phases. Record the decision and the reasons, whichever way it goes.

## 6. Fix the cause (after)

A work account for the people who need one, clear rules about which data may go in, and a short word with the team.

One incident is usually the first one you hear about, not the only one. The AI accounts guide has a 20-minute check of which accounts your team really uses.

## Factors that point towards reporting

- **Who received it.** A personal account has no processor agreement with your firm, so the vendor isn't bound by your instructions about this data.
- **Can it be undone.** Training was on, or may have been. The vendor may use the data to train its models, and deleting the chat may not undo that.
- **Is it a breach.** The Dutch authority says personal data entered into a chatbot against the employer's agreements is a data breach.
- **Sensitivity of the data.** Health, other special categories or criminal data: the harm to the people concerned can be especially severe.
- **Sensitivity of the data.** Identity numbers or financial details can cause harm on their own, and together they can be used for identity theft.
- **Who the people are.** Data about children: the people concerned may be at greater risk.
- **Sensitivity of the data.** CVs and assessments often reveal more about a person than they seem to.
- **Severity of the consequences.** Passwords or keys open other systems. Until they are changed, the risk reaches everything they give access to.
- **Number of people.** More people affected generally means more impact.
- **How far it spread.** A share link may let anyone who has it read the chat. Remove it and record when.
- **If in doubt.** You can't rule out sensitive data yet. The European guidance is to report when in doubt.

## Factors that may lower the risk

- **Who received it.** A work account under a processor agreement binds the vendor to your firm's instructions, if your firm accepted or signed that agreement. A recipient you can rely on can lower the risk; it doesn't mean nothing happened.
- **Is it a breach.** Within your own rules, the Dutch authority doesn't treat chatbot use as a data breach, though it says such use is often not permitted by law. Record it and check the basis for the use.
- **Sensitivity of the data.** Only names and contact details of a few people. In ordinary circumstances that is unlikely to cause substantial damage, though context can change that.
- **Is it a breach.** No personal data: this is not a personal data breach under the GDPR. Check your client contract's confidentiality clause all the same.
- **Can it be undone.** Deleted, with training off: this lowers the chance of further use. It doesn't undo what the vendor has already received.

## Questions

### Do we have to report it to the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens?

Only you can decide that. The rule: report within 72 hours of becoming aware, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the people concerned. The Dutch authority says reporting is mandatory in many chatbot cases. The European guidance says that if in doubt, you should report.

### When do the 72 hours start?

When your firm has a reasonable degree of certainty that personal data was compromised. A short first investigation to establish that doesn't count, but it should start straight away.

### We handle this data for a client. Who reports?

If you are the client's processor, your duty is to tell the client without undue delay. You don't have to assess the risk first; the client does that and decides about reporting. Your processing agreement may set a shorter deadline.

### We decided not to report. Do we still need a record?

Yes. The GDPR requires a record of every personal data breach, including the facts, the effects and what you did. The authority can ask to see it.

### Was it a breach if the employee used a work account we pay for?

If using it with this data was within your rules, the Dutch authority doesn't treat it as a data breach, though it says such use is often not permitted by law. Record what happened and check that the use had a proper basis.
